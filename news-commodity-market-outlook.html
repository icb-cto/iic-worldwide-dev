Commodities Rally Over 30% in 2026 as Supply Risks Reshape Global Markets

Geopolitical tensions and supply chain disruptions drive a broad-based commodity surge, with energy prices projected to rise 24% to their highest level since 2022.

Source: S&P Global / Bloomberg September 3, 2026 London, UK

Commodities have emerged as one of the standout asset classes of 2026, with broad-based indices surging approximately 31% year-to-date. The rally has been driven by a convergence of geopolitical instability, supply chain reconfiguration, and the accelerating global race toward decarbonization — all of which have tightened supply across multiple commodity categories.

Energy Leads the Surge

Energy prices are projected to climb 24% in 2026 to their highest levels since Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022. The Middle East conflict has sent a severe shock through global energy markets, with oil and natural gas prices responding to disruption risks across key shipping lanes and production regions.

However, the energy picture is not uniformly bullish. The global LNG market is heading toward a supply glut, with new production capacity coming online from the United States, Qatar, Australia, Mexico, and several African nations. This divergence between oil and gas markets is creating a complex landscape for energy traders.

Commodities are up around 31% in 2026, yet the average financial adviser holds only 4.6% of client money in the sector — a striking disconnect that suggests institutional allocation has not yet caught up with the market reality.

Industrial Metals in Focus

Industrial metals are expected to outperform precious metals through the remainder of 2026, supported by supply deficits, rising infrastructure investment, and post-tariff growth dynamics. Copper, aluminum, and nickel prices have all benefited from increased demand driven by electrification and renewable energy build-out.

At the same time, critical mineral investment declined 9% in 2025, ending several years of continuous growth. Lithium companies reduced investment by around 40%, while broader battery-metal capital expenditure fell by more than 20%. This investment pullback, if sustained, could tighten supply further and push prices higher in coming years.

Policy Shifts and Supply Chain Reconfigurations

The commodity rally is also being fueled by structural changes in global trade patterns. Nearshoring trends, tariff uncertainty, and evolving trade alliances are reshaping how commodities flow between regions. Deutsche Bank's commodities outlook notes that these policy-driven shifts are creating both risks and opportunities for market participants.

The World Bank's Commodity Markets Outlook highlights that fertilizer prices have reached record highs alongside key metals, adding cost pressure to agricultural production and potentially affecting food security in vulnerable regions.
