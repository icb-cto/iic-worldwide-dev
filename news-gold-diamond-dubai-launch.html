Gold Retreats Over 21% From Record High as Rate-Hike Expectations Grow

After reaching a record $5,589 per ounce in January, gold has pulled back to around $4,370 as markets price in a near 60% probability of a Federal Reserve rate hike.

Source: CBS News / Yahoo Finance September 4, 2026 New York, USA

Gold prices have experienced a significant correction in 2026, falling more than 21% from their all-time high. After climbing to a record $5,589.38 per ounce on January 28, the precious metal reversed course and by early September sits at approximately $4,370 per ounce — a decline of roughly $1,220 from its peak.

Federal Reserve Policy Drives the Selloff

The primary catalyst behind gold's retreat has been a shift in monetary policy expectations. Resilient labor market data throughout 2026 has strengthened the case for tighter monetary policy, with money markets now pricing in a near 60% probability of a Federal Reserve rate hike in September.

However, dovish comments from Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller briefly tempered those expectations, leading traders to scale back the probability of a September hike to roughly 50%. This policy tug-of-war has created significant volatility in gold markets, with prices swinging on each new economic data release.

Gold's 21.8% decline from its January peak reflects a fundamental repricing as markets adjust to a higher-for-longer interest rate environment that diminishes the appeal of non-yielding assets.

Geopolitical Tensions Provide a Floor

While monetary policy has pushed gold lower, escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have prevented an even steeper decline. The U.S. military carried out further attacks on Iranian targets in early September, with Iran retaliating with strikes on U.S. military bases in the region. These exchanges represent a significant re-escalation of the U.S.-Iran conflict and have supported safe-haven demand for gold.

Where Does Gold Go From Here?

Analysts remain divided on gold's near-term trajectory. For September 2026, forecasts range widely from $4,136 to $5,304 per ounce. By month's end, the consensus projects gold at around $5,051, while more conservative estimates place it at approximately $4,444.

Key factors to watch include the Federal Reserve's September policy decision, further developments in the U.S.-Iran situation, and upcoming employment and inflation data that could shift rate-hike expectations in either direction.

Gold December futures opened at $4,522 per troy ounce on Friday, September 4, indicating some recovery from the week's lows as traders positioned ahead of the monthly jobs report.
